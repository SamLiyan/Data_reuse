Non-host reads in blue catfish reproductive tissue
============================================================================

Species : Ictalurus furcatus (both studies)
Studies : PRJNA1301936  testis, 14 runs
          GSE296208 / PRJNA1258209  sperm, 60 runs (30 males, fresh + cryo)
Measure : per-run taxonomic composition of each library

1. HOW MUCH OF EACH LIBRARY IS NOT FISH?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  tissue       n  bacteria % min    median       max  viruses max
  testis      14            0.63      2.24      3.04       0.0008
  sperm       60            0.18     17.61     85.59       0.0056

  testis vs sperm, Mann-Whitney U=108, p=1.71e-05

  Viruses and fungi are negligible everywhere (max 0.0056% and 0.0026%).
  Whatever is in these libraries besides fish is bacterial.

  Widest spread is in sperm: 0.18% to 85.6%, a 483-fold range.
  SRR33411471 is 85.6% bacterial and 4.7% metazoan. It is deposited without comment.

2. IS IT THE SAME ORGANISM IN EVERY SAMPLE?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  A signal introduced by shared handling is the same genus everywhere. A
  signal carried by the animal differs between individuals.

  testis   most abundant genus is Methylobacterium in 5/14 runs; 9 distinct genera take the top slot
  sperm    most abundant genus is Parabacteroides in 13/60 runs; 14 distinct genera take the top slot

  Neither study is dominated by one organism. In sperm the top genus is
  Parabacteroides in only 13/60 libraries, and the
  identity of that genus turns out to depend on how much there is (section 3).

3. WHAT KIND OF ORGANISMS, AND DOES IT TRACK THE AMOUNT?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Reference lists were fixed before this analysis (data/*_genera.csv).

                            environment    gut   other
  testis                              7      5       2
  sperm                              30     13      17

  In sperm, split by whether the library carries more or less than 30% bacterial:

                          environment    gut   other
  <= 30% bacterial                 19      3      15
   > 30% bacterial                 11     10       2

  Fisher exact p = 0.0026, odds ratio 8.7
  Libraries topped by a gut genus carry a median 48.6% bacterial reads;
  those topped by an environment genus, 18.0% (Mann-Whitney p = 0.031).

  Parabacteroides, a gut anaerobe, is the most abundant genus in 13 of 60 sperm
  libraries. The lightly loaded libraries are topped by Stenotrophomonas,
  Pseudomonas, Staphylococcus, Cutibacterium and Stutzerimonas.

  Alistipes, Blautia and Escherichia each top one library and are gut
  organisms in the literature, but were not on the list fixed beforehand, so
  they are counted under 'other' rather than moved across after the fact.

4. THE SAME MALE, SAMPLED TWICE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  30 males were each sequenced fresh and cryopreserved.

    fresh         median 17.22%  (range 0.18 - 79.26)
    cryopreserved median 18.05%  (range 0.27 - 85.59)

    paired Wilcoxon, fresh vs cryo : p = 0.685   no effect of freezing
    Spearman, within the same male : rho = +0.742, p = 2.6e-06

  Two libraries prepared separately from the same fish agree on how much of
  the library is not fish. The quantity belongs to the male, not to the
  handling, and it is therefore measured twice rather than once.

5. DOES IT TRACK OFFSPRING PERFORMANCE?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Crosses were made with FRESH milt, so the 30 fresh libraries are the
  analysis. The cryopreserved libraries come from the same 30 males and are
  tabulated alongside for completeness as a repeat measurement of each
  male's non-host fraction.

      libraries               trait  n  spearman_rho  spearman_p  slope_per_10x    r2  ols_p
fresh (primary)       hatch_success 30        -0.096      0.6142           0.62 0.001 0.8616
fresh (primary) larval_survival_30d 30         0.331      0.0741           2.24 0.060 0.1905
  cryo (repeat)       hatch_success 30         0.183      0.3340           4.16 0.054 0.2178
  cryo (repeat) larval_survival_30d 30         0.401      0.0282           2.79 0.101 0.0876

  Hatch success          rho = -0.096, p = 0.614, r2 = 0.001
    No relationship. A male at 0.18% non-host reads and a male at 79% hatch
    about equally well. Three orders of magnitude in the predictor explain
    none of the variance.

  Larval survival 30 d   rho = +0.331, p = 0.074, r2 = 0.060
    Also not significant. The correlation is weak and does not reach
    p < 0.05 at n = 30, so no relationship is claimed for this trait
    either. The cryopreserved libraries are tabulated above for
    completeness, but the crosses were made with fresh milt, so they are
    not the test.

  CONFOUNDERS
    sequencing depth vs non-host fraction : rho = -0.014, p = 0.941
    host fraction vs non-host fraction    : rho = -0.992, p = 1.0e-26
    hatch success vs larval survival      : rho = +0.466, p = 0.009

    The second line is the limit on all of this. Within one library, 'more
    bacterial reads' and 'less fish RNA recovered' are the same measurement.
    Nothing in a read fraction can separate a larger microbial population
    from a poorer recovery of host RNA.

    Scope: one species, one season, n = 30, four tests, no correction for
    multiple testing. Neither trait shows a relationship that holds at this
    sample size. The non-host fraction varies enormously between males and
    is reproducible within a male, but it does not predict either outcome
    measured here.
